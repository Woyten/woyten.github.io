1. The claim we started with

The fine-structure constant \(\alpha \approx 1/137\) sets the strength of the electromagnetic interaction. In the Standard Model it is measured, not predicted. This document reports a candidate closed form for it and the theory that grew out of the closed form — a theory whose nature is different from the Standard Model's (chapter 3), but which survives being confronted with the same data.

CODATA 2022: \(1/\alpha = 137.035999177(21)\). Deviation \(2.07\times10^{-8}\) in \(1/\alpha\), a relative error of \(3.75\times10^{-11}\) — 0.245σ, inside the measurement uncertainty. Every constant on the right is canonical: \(\pi\), the natural base \(e\), \(\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6\), \(\sqrt3\), the rational \(21/20\), and Catalan's constant \(G\), the value of the Dirichlet beta function \(\beta\) at \(s=2\).

Tens of millions of combinations were evaluated in exact rational and high-precision floating arithmetic. Every widening of the search then found no competing form: within the enumerated space the match is unique.

3. The theory, and why it was built that way

  • \(\zeta(s)\beta(s)^{2}\) is a counting function of a crystal. Counting functions belong to structures that have crystals. So the theory took the crystal as fundamental rather than as a regulator.
  • \(e^{4}\) is a response factor: the moment generating function of a measurement. Responses belong to measurement. So the theory took measurement as primitive — the reading of a system, not a classical state.

  • \(7/40\) is a census: a finite inventory of trace products. Censuses belong to finite algebras. So the theory treated the Standard Model's gauge groups and matter bookkeeping as emergent from the same finite algebra, not postulated.

The result is a theory of a genre between condensed-matter physics and the operator-algebraic constructions of mathematical physics: an arithmetic lattice theory with emergent gauge structure.
